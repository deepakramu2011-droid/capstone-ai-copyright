# Verification Report: Copyright & Licensing Guide

**Checked:** 3 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Demonstrate source verification and correction of outdated or overbroad claims. This is not a legal opinion.

## Checks and corrections

| Risky draft claim or source | What the official source says | Safer correction |
|---|---|---|
| “The 2023 Google Generative AI Additional Terms are the current Gemini terms.” | The official page says Google updated its general Terms on 22 May 2024 to cover AI-related topics. The standalone 2023 additional terms no longer apply to ordinary use unless a signed business agreement references them. [1] | Use the current Google Terms and service-specific notices for the relevant account. Mark the 2023 page as archived/limited. |
| “The GitHub Copilot Business product terms cover every Copilot user.” | The archived product-specific terms say they were deprecated for subscriptions/renewals from 5 March 2026. GitHub's current standard Terms AI Features section applies to individual users unless a separate contract governs. [2] [3] | Identify whether the account is personal or business, and cite the matching terms. |
| “An output ownership assignment means a student may copyright and sell any output.” | U.S. Copyright Office analysis requires sufficient human authorship for protection, while provider terms separately allocate contractual interests. Similar outputs and third-party rights can remain. [4] [5] | Separate provider contract, copyrightability, third-party rights, and intended-use terms. Verify each. |
| “India's computer-generated author clause settles modern AI authorship.” | Section 2(d)(vi) of India's Copyright Act defines author for certain computer-generated works, and section 17 addresses first ownership. The statutory text predates current generative AI systems. [6] | Quote the text accurately and frame application to current AI as an interpretive question, not a settled universal answer. |
| “CC licensing lets creators control every training use.” | Creative Commons explains that a CC licence grants permissions for rights covered by copyright and does not override exceptions/limitations or address other legal areas such as privacy. [7] | Explain licence scope and limitations; do not promise that CC text blocks every training use. |

## Before / after analysis

**Before:** “AI content belongs to the person who typed the prompt. It is safe to use commercially if the tool says so.”

**After:** “A provider may assign rights it has or disclaim ownership, but this does not settle local copyrightability or clear third-party rights. Check the current service and plan terms, source licences, similarity risks, and local law. Record meaningful human edits and get qualified advice if the release is high-stakes.”

## Verification method

The corrections above were checked against official regulator, legislation, platform-terms, and licence sources. Search snippets were not treated as proof; the source pages or official documents were opened and read. URLs and claim-to-source references are in `report.md`. The dated comparison must be refreshed before a later submission because product terms, feature access, and legal guidance can change.
