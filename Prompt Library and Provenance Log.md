# Prompt Library and Provenance Log

These prompts support research and review. They do not ask an AI model to decide whether a use is lawful. Verify claims against official sources yourself.

## Research and writing prompts

1. **Plan the research** — “Build a source plan for [question]. Prioritize official statutes, courts, regulator pages, licence texts, and tool terms. Separate established rules from uncertain issues. Do not invent sources.”
2. **Summarize a source** — “Summarize this official page for CSE students in 100 words. Identify the exact sections supporting each sentence. Preserve uncertainty and do not add unsupported advice.”
3. **Compare terms** — “Compare the exact output, input, data-use, accuracy, and third-party-rights clauses in these terms. Identify plan scope and date. Do not infer a commercial-use right unless the text says so.”
4. **Explain a licence** — “Explain this licence's conditions for modification and distribution using the actual clauses. Note where project facts affect the answer. Do not call it ‘free for anything.’”
5. **Check a code snippet** — “Review this code for tests, security problems, copied-looking distinctive fragments, and missing notices. Do not call it original or licence-free. Tell me how to verify possible matches.”
6. **Check a visual** — “List visible brands, characters, public figures, and distinctive elements that may require review. This is a screening list, not a clearance decision.”
7. **Find counter-evidence** — “Try to falsify each claim. For every claim, find a current official source and state its date, scope, and what it does not prove.”
8. **Draft an AI-use note** — “Using only this process log, draft a short factual disclosure. Do not say AI was not used, do not claim a detector was passed, and do not make legal conclusions about copyright ownership.”
9. **Build a quiz** — “Create ten scenario-based questions using only the supplied sources. Add an answer key with source references. Mark unsupported questions instead of guessing.”
