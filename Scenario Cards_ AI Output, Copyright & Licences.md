# Scenario Cards: AI Output, Copyright & Licences

Use each card as a short discussion prompt. The suggested answer is a cautious next step, not a legal ruling.

## 1. Prompt-only poster

**Situation:** A student enters “make a futuristic campus poster,” accepts the first image, and publishes it in a portfolio.

**Discuss:** What human creative choices are visible? What rights might still belong to others?

**Safer move:** Document real edits and layout choices, check tool terms, and review recognizable characters, brands, and style imitation. In the U.S. Copyright Office's 2025 analysis, prompts alone generally did not provide sufficient expressive control; human contributions are assessed case by case. [1]

## 2. Code that looks familiar

**Situation:** An AI coding tool outputs a distinctive function with unusual comments.

**Safer move:** Search exact fragments, inspect any matching source and its licence, remove or rewrite uncertain material, preserve required notices, then test and review security. GitHub's personal terms warn that output may resemble training-set code and place review/licensing responsibility on the user. [2]

## 3. MIT dependency

**Situation:** A project imports an MIT-licensed package.

**Safer move:** Preserve copyright and permission notices in copies or substantial portions, record the package version, and inspect its transitive dependencies. [3]

## 4. GPL component in a distributed app

**Situation:** A student bundles a GPL-3.0 library into an application they intend to distribute.

**Safer move:** Review the exact combination and distribution model. GPL-3.0 can require source and licensing conditions for covered works; whether the project is a covered work or an aggregate depends on facts. Get qualified review where release plans depend on the answer. [4]

## 5. CC BY-NC art in a monetized video

**Situation:** A creator credits a CC BY-NC image at the end of a video that earns ad revenue.

**Safer move:** Credit is not enough to remove the non-commercial restriction. Find an asset with commercial permission or obtain permission from the rights holder. [5]

## 6. Unpublished teammate code in a prompt

**Situation:** A student pastes an unreleased team repository into a personal AI service for debugging.

**Safer move:** Ask the teammate, remove secrets and personal data, check the course and service policies, and use an approved tool. The submitter must have the rights and permissions required by the provider terms. [6] [7]
