# Ten-Question Quiz: AI Copyright & Licensing

## Questions

1. If an AI provider says it does not claim ownership of an output, does this prove the output is copyrightable?
2. According to the 2025 U.S. Copyright Office Part 2 report, are prompts alone generally enough to establish authorship of expressive output?
3. What notice must generally remain when distributing MIT-licensed software?
4. Give one condition associated with conveying a GPL-3.0 covered modified work.
5. Is a public repository automatically open source?
6. What should you do if generated code closely matches a distinctive existing snippet?
7. Do EU AI Act provider transparency obligations give a student permission to copy a protected work?
8. Why might a monetized video using a CC BY-NC image need permission beyond attribution?
9. What should you do before pasting unpublished teammate code into a consumer AI service?
10. Name two items to include in an AI prompt/provenance log.

## Answer key

1. No. Contract ownership and copyrightability are separate questions; third-party rights and local law still matter.
2. The Office says prompts alone, with generally available technology at the time of the report, do not provide sufficient control. Human contributions are reviewed case by case. [1]
3. Keep the copyright notice and permission notice in copies or substantial portions. [2]
4. Examples: mark changes, preserve notices, license the covered modified work under GPL-3.0, and provide or offer corresponding source as required. [3]
5. No. Reuse depends on an actual licence or permission.
6. Search the exact distinctive fragment, review the source and its licence, remove/rewrite/obtain permission if unclear, and test the remaining code.
7. No. Provider-facing transparency requirements do not grant an end-user licence to third-party material. [4]
8. CC BY-NC restricts certain commercial uses; attribution does not cancel the non-commercial condition. [5]
9. Get permission, check institutional and service policy, remove secrets/personal data, or choose an approved tool.
10. Examples: tool/model and date; prompt; output segment kept; human edits; source URL; licence/version; reviewer/test result.
